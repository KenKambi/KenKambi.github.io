**#OSM911** was our team name! Together with [Joseph Munyenze](https://www.linkedin.com/in/joseph-munyenze-b836851b5/) and [Mary Muthee](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mary-muthee-b91932201/), we participated in the OSM **Kathon** competition. 🏆

Even though we did not make it to the top 3, we are glad that our project was [featured by the team behind the Kathon!](https://www.useosm.org/en/blogs/mapkathon-2026-winners) 🎉

## 🚨 The Question

We built a **disaster response portal for Mozambique** with a simple goal: to help answer one critical question during an emergency.

**Where is the nearest help, and how long will it take to get there?**

## 🗺️ What the Portal Does

Users can select any location in Mozambique by **dropping a pin** or **searching for a place**. The portal then identifies the closest facilities across key response categories:

1. 🏥 Health facilities
2. 🏠 Shelters
3. 🚒 Rescue services
4. 💧 WASH services
5. 🚌 Transport facilities

Users can also **visualize road routes and estimated travel times** to these facilities, and **export the assessment as a bilingual PDF briefing** for easier communication and decision-making.

## 🌍 Built for the People Who Need It

To make sure the application benefits locals, we integrated a **bilingual feature**. The application is accessible in **Portuguese** (the local language) and in **English**.

## 🎯 Takeaways

Hackathons move fast, and this one was a great reminder of what open data can do. Taking OpenStreetMap data and turning it into something that could help people find help during an emergency is exactly the kind of work I enjoy at the intersection of mapping and open-source development.

Placing in the top 3 would have been nice, but being featured by the organizers and building something we are proud of made it worthwhile. 🙌

Have fun interacting with the dashboard! 👇
